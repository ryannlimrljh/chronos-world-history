# Dates needing a historian's eye

Entries below are in the dataset with honest `precision`/`confidence` flags,
but the specific numbers deserve expert review before anyone treats this
chart as citable. Grouped by how shaky they are.

## Conventional-but-contested framework dates
These follow one common scholarly convention where several exist.
- **Egyptian chronology** (old/middle/new kingdom, intermediate periods):
  all dates follow the conventional chronology; low vs high chronology
  shifts everything by decades.
- **Mesopotamian early dates** (sumer start -4000, akkadian -2334, ur-iii,
  old-babylonian -1894): middle chronology used; short chronology moves
  Hammurabi ~64 years later.
- **xia-erlitou** (-1900..-1600): identification of Erlitou with Xia is
  itself disputed, hence category "disputed" and confidence "low".
- **shang** start -1600: Xia–Shang–Zhou chronology project value, contested.
- **gojoseon** start -700: legendary founding is -2333; archaeology
  supports a state by the 7th c. BCE at earliest. Start date is a floor,
  not a fact.
- **israel-judah** start -1047: the United Monarchy's existence and dating
  are actively debated; -586 (fall of Jerusalem) is solid.

## Culture-spans standing in for states
These are archaeological cultures or processes, not polities, included so
their lanes aren't empty. Their spans are envelope dates.
- caral, poverty-point, olmec, chavin, teotihuacan, tiwanaku,
  mississippian, lapita, polynesian-expansion, andronovo, scythians,
  celtic-tribes, germanic-tribes, dong-son, vedic-period, canaan-phoenicia.

## Individual dates I'd want checked
- **maya end 1546**: chose the (rough) end of the Spanish conquest of
  Yucatán; last independent kingdom (Nojpetén) actually fell 1697.
- **tui-tonga 950**: oral-tradition based; scholarship varies by centuries.
- **maori-iwi start 1320**: settlement of Aotearoa ~1250-1350 per current
  radiocarbon work; 1320 is a midpoint.
- **ghana-empire start 300**: varies by source from 300 to 700 CE.
- **kanem-bornu start 700**: first dynasty dates are traditional.
- **benin-kingdom start 1180**: dynastic tradition, not documentary.
- **great-zimbabwe 1220-1450**: occupation dates vary by excavator.
- **kush start -1070**: Napatan state formation is gradual; -1070 marks
  Egyptian withdrawal, not a founding.
- **aksum 100-940**: both ends soft by a century.
- **funan 68**: from Chinese sources; local evidence starts later.
- **srivijaya 650-1275**: both ends approximate; polity may have been a
  shifting alliance rather than a state.
- **khmer end 1431**: sack of Angkor; the kingdom persisted at Phnom Penh.
  Chose the conventional "end of empire" date.
- **venice start 697**: the first-doge date is traditional, likely later.
- **wessex start 519**: Anglo-Saxon Chronicle backdating, treated as
  "disputed".
- **kofun-yamato start 250**: tomb-period start varies 200-300 CE.
- **alaouite-morocco start 1631**: dynasty's rise was gradual through the
  1630s-1660s.
- **hittite start -1650**: middle chronology.
- **satavahana start -100**: foundational dates range -230 to -30 across
  sources, hence "disputed".

## Structural simplifications a historian may object to
- Chinese interregna (Three Kingdoms 220-266, Northern & Southern
  dynasties 420-581, Five Dynasties 907-960) are currently gaps between
  major dynasties rather than entries. v1 should add them.
- "Korea" and "China (Republic & PRC)" compress divided/successive states
  into single modern entries.
- Roman Gaul & Britannia, Egyptian Nubia, Ottoman & Khedival Egypt are
  colonial-occupation spans standing in for local political detail.
